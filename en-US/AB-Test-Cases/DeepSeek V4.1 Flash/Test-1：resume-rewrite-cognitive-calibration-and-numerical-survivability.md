### 0.3 Reality Conditions List

1. **Resume survivability.** Every number and every verb on the resume is the interviewer's question list. A strong sentence that cannot withstand follow-up questioning has negative value in an interview — it builds trust first, then destroys it.

2. **Screening funnel.** Initial screening is a 6–10 second scan; the first three lines and section headers carry the entire positioning load. Then the hiring manager reads. Then interview line-by-line interrogation. Three gates with different evaluators and criteria, in sequence.

3. **Institutional trust gap: agency → brand-side.** Three default suspicions: unclear decision authority, unclear outcome attribution (you or the budget), breadth traded for depth. These won't be asked out loud — they directly determine whether an interview is scheduled.

4. **Boundary between material and inference.** The user provided results and roles, not method details. Standard practices for digital marketing professionals — audience segmentation, creative testing iteration, awareness/interest/conversion three-tier funnel, cross-account reporting in multi-client settings — carry near-zero challenge risk when added to a resume, because they're almost certainly true, and "making abstract descriptions concrete" is precisely what the user said they can't do, which is why they delegated it. What needs verification is additions without industry-default backing: claiming a system was established, claiming a structural before-and-after change occurred, claiming a never-stated condition holds true. **The boundary is not whether something exceeds the material, but whether it has an industry default to lean on.**

5. **Reverse interpretability of numbers.** CPA reduction can be achieved by volume reduction (standard follow-up: concurrent conversion volume); follower count is widely regarded as a vanity metric (standard follow-up: contribution to revenue); ROAS has enormous category-baseline and account-scope variance; revenue growth needs to distinguish input increase from efficiency improvement; percentage growth needs absolute base.

6. **Role-level reality.** Five years of experience, leading two people, agency background, four core competencies missing from the material (annual budget planning / cross-departmental collaboration / channel pricing / CRM membership) → brand-side marketing manager is a level-up application. Acceptance varies by company type: D2C / startups / small-to-mid consumer brands are highly feasible (those roles want exactly "one person running the entire line"), chain retail and F&B are moderate, large consumer goods groups are difficult.

7. **Confidentiality clauses.** NDAs are pervasive in agency work; the disclosure risk of four brand names on a resume depends on their separation agreement.

8. **Number consistency.** Monthly budget NT$1.5–2.5M → annual range NT$18–30M.

9. **Usage behavior.** "Don't know how to work them in" means they will adopt a high proportion of the response text directly. Unlabeled inferences will enter the resume as-is; content flagged as needing confirmation will trigger their verification action.

10. **Job search as multi-round use.** They'll submit multiple applications, face different JDs, and bring back interview feedback. The rewrite is not the endpoint — they need to be able to revise it themselves.

11. **Timeline inconsistency.** The material says "2020–2025"; the current date is Q3 2026, leaving at least eight months unaccounted for, and they submitted it as-is — they haven't run "how will this be read" simulation on their own document.
